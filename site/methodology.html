What GippyRank is doing under the hood

GippyRank is a probabilistic college-football ranking system. It starts with uncertainty about each team, updates that uncertainty with score-margin evidence, and keeps separate distributions for team quality, individual-game performance, future games, and season outcomes.

The short answer

The rankings are not a poll, a résumé table, or an Elo number. They are summaries of posterior probability distributions over rank-like latent states. The table orders teams by expected rank, while the distribution behind each team says how much uncertainty remains.

01

What GippyRank is trying to measure

The main question is: given the evidence available at a snapshot, how strong might this team actually be? “Strong” is represented by an uncertain final ordinal rank in the team’s modeled subdivision. Lower rank numbers mean stronger teams.

That makes GippyRank different from several familiar objects:

  • It is not a résumé or standings ranking. Those ask what a team has accomplished or what its record is.
  • It is not a poll or a report of what voters believe.
  • It is not a single point-differential table or a deterministic power number.
  • It is not an Elo clone with one scalar rating per team.

GippyRank keeps three ideas separate: a team’s underlying quality, the randomness of one game’s result, and the performance implied by one completed game. A dominant win can be evidence about quality, but the game-performance distribution is not itself the team’s season-long posterior.

Interpretive rule

Use Predictive rankings for “who does the model think is strongest?” Use Performance rankings for “who has played best so far?” Use Schedule and team pages to see the uncertainty behind either answer.

02

The latent team-quality model

For each team, the latent variable is its final ordinal rank in its subdivision. A team does not receive one certain rank internally; it receives a discrete probability mass function, or PMF, over possible ranks.

rᵢ ∈ {1, …, N}   and   P(rᵢ | evidence)

For FBS teams, the published distribution is over the FBS rank support. FCS rank states are used when the model needs a supported cross-subdivision opponent representation. The rank coordinate is rank-percentile based, but the emitted result remains a discrete PMF over ordinal ranks.

What the site summarizes

  • Expected rank: the probability-weighted average rank.
  • Median and mode: useful points in a potentially asymmetric PMF.
  • Intervals: central 50%, 80%, and 95% probability ranges.
  • Top-N probabilities: the mass assigned to ranks 1–5, 1–10, or 1–25.

Technical detail: the rank coordinate

For a population of N teams and ordinal rank r, the likelihood uses (r − 0.5) / N as the rank-percentile coordinate. Lower coordinates correspond to stronger ranks. The coordinate makes smooth score-margin surfaces possible without introducing a separate strength variable.

03

Preseason priors

A prior is the model’s distribution before the target season’s eligible game evidence is applied. The two production prior families are alternative starting points for the same posterior engine.

Primary predictive family

Predictive Context C 1.2

Context starts with rank history and adds approved preseason context: coaching tenure, recruiting class rank and points, multi-year recruiting summaries and trend, team-talent composite, and returning production measures for total, passing, receiving, and rushing production.

These inputs are preseason-semantic facts or historical rank summaries. They are not target-season game outcomes. Missing context uses training-only median imputation plus missingness indicators; rank-history cold starts retain the History fallback.

Alternative prior family

Predictive History H 1.1

History uses rank history only. Its production specification is a long-run baseline built from prior-season rank distributions, including lagged and long-run summaries. It deliberately does not add recruiting, roster, talent, or coaching inputs.

History is a sensitivity view of prior assumptions, not a second likelihood or a blend with Context.

Both priors are distributions, not point estimates. They carry uncertainty from the observed rank histories and preseason inputs into the later posterior and prediction calculations.

Technical detail: prior fitting and cutoff safety

The prior specifications are fit on earlier seasons and evaluated on temporally held-out seasons. Context inputs use a conservative August 15 preseason cutoff convention for the target season. The annual target-season artifacts prohibit target outcomes; a retrospective reconstruction is not treated as a contemporaneous source capture.

04

Historical Likelihood V1

The Historical Likelihood describes how plausible an observed score margin is for a pair of rank states, given the site and subdivision pairing:

P(game result | rhome, raway, site, pairing)

For same-subdivision games, the observed margin is home points minus away points. For an FBS–FCS game, the model’s stable cross-subdivision response is FBS points minus FCS points, regardless of which team is listed first. The browser-facing future prediction converts the result back to a canonical home-minus-away margin.

Historical Likelihood V1 treatment by game context
ContextEvidence orientationSite treatment
FBS vs FBSHome minus away marginNon-neutral home-field indicator or neutral reference
FCS vs FCSHome minus away marginSame-subdivision site treatment
FBS vs FCSFBS minus FCS marginFBS-home, FCS-home, or neutral indicators

The residual distribution is Student-t with 15 degrees of freedom. Heavy tails are useful for football because score margins include occasional blowouts, unusual game states, and other observations that a thin-tailed residual model would treat as impossibly influential. The frozen production surface uses the weighted-pseudo eight-iteration IRLS fit and a serialized scale.

Point differential is therefore evidence inside a likelihood; it is not a rule that directly awards a fixed number of ranking points. The production likelihood does not use YPP, betting lines, polls, injuries, or a direct win/loss bonus.

Technical detail: the fitted surface

The same-subdivision surface uses a smooth odd basis in the two rank-percentile coordinates: difference terms, difference-by-mean terms, a magnitude term, hinge terms, and non-neutral home field. The cross-subdivision surface uses FBS-first coordinates with linear, quadratic, interaction, hinge, and home-side indicators. The coefficient artifact and its provenance are maintained in the repository; the public contract is the versioned surface and its semantics.

05

Posterior rankings

Bayesian updating can be stated simply:

posterior ∝ prior × likelihood

For a season, the model multiplies the prior for every team by one pairwise game factor for every eligible completed game. Multiple games combine as evidence about the network of teams, so an opponent’s inferred quality affects how the model interprets a result.

The production implementation uses deterministic, damped loopy sum-product belief propagation. It passes messages through the schedule graph so evidence can travel beyond direct opponents. In a tree, this calculation is exact. Real schedules contain cycles, so the published marginals are an approximation; the inference layer requires convergence before a snapshot can be marked valid.

  • Completed eligible games update team-quality beliefs.
  • Uncertainty can contract, move, or remain broad as evidence accumulates.
  • Games within a selected cutoff are not down-weighted by chronology in the base season-wide model.
  • Rematches are consolidated for message passing, while individual game IDs remain available for game-performance explanations.
Approximation caveat

Posterior PMFs are marginal distributions. They are not a sampled joint permutation of all teams, and expected ranks should not be read as a deterministic ordinal truth.

Technical detail: why the graph matters

A pairwise factor connects two rank coordinates. A message to one team summarizes the other team’s prior and all neighboring factors except the current pair. The algorithm iterates these messages with damping, then normalizes each team’s marginal. This is why later evidence about an opponent can change the interpretation of an earlier game.

07

Performance rankings

Performance is a distinct ranking family designed to answer a different question: who has performed best in the eligible games so far? It is not the same as asking who is strongest or who has the best résumé.

Production Performance 1.0 is Context-anchored and uses the prior_stripping method. It begins with the Context posterior from the complete schedule network and removes the focal team’s Context preseason prior as a direct factor:

Performanceᵢ(r) = normalize(PosteriorContext,i(r) / ContextPriorᵢ(r))

Opponent quality still needs to be estimated, so Context remains the anchor for the other teams in the network. Under loopy inference, cycles can leave a small indirect feedback residue; the repository audits the direct factor-removal construction against explicit focal-prior neutralization.

  • Performance uses completed eligible games, their margins, sites, and current opponent beliefs.
  • A team with no eligible game receives a neutral uniform PMF and is shown as NR.
  • There is no win bonus, loss penalty, quality-win bonus, capped margin, or postseason selection rule.
  • Future predictions on Performance pages use Predictive Context; completed-game ratings also remain Context-anchored.
Predictive asks

What does the model think this team’s underlying quality might be?

Performance asks

What quality does the football this team has played so far imply?

08

Individual game-performance ratings

A completed-game rating is an explanatory distribution for one team in one game. It combines the game’s Historical Likelihood factor with the opponent’s pair-cavity belief:

game performance PMF = normalize(game likelihood × opponent cavity belief)

That makes the rating opponent-adjusted and site-adjusted. It is not raw margin. A close road loss to a strong opponent can look like a good performance; a close home win over a weak opponent can look ordinary.

Individual game-performance display fields
FieldMeaning
Expected / median / intervalSummaries of the game-performance PMF; rank 1 is best and rank N is worst.
PercentileEmpirical position among eligible FBS team-game performances in the same snapshot reference population; lower expected rank is better.
GradePresentation shorthand derived from percentile, not an input or model output.

An 88th-percentile performance rates better than about 88% of eligible reference performances. Exact ties receive half credit. The grade thresholds are A ≥ 90, B ≥ 70, C ≥ 30, D ≥ 10, and F < 10. The chart is a compact display of the PMF; exact summaries remain authoritative.

09

Future-game predictions

For a scheduled game, the model integrates over both teams’ posterior PMFs rather than plugging in their expected ranks:

P(M | snapshot) = ΣᵢΣⱼ P(M | rhome, raway, site, pairing) × P(rhome | snapshot) × P(raway | snapshot)

Each rank pair contributes a Student-t margin distribution. The mixture therefore includes both uncertainty about team quality and game-level randomness.

The prediction artifact contains expected margin, median margin, win probabilities, tie probability, and central 50%, 80%, and 95% margin intervals. The canonical browser orientation is home points minus away points. Neutral games retain a neutral site row rather than inventing home-field advantage.

The Student-t mixture is continuous, so production tie probability is zero. The contract still uses the explicit football convention: a point mass at exactly zero, if a future likelihood ever supplies one, receives half credit for each team.

A future prediction is shown only when the kickoff is after the selected effective cutoff, the game is not already included as evidence, and both teams have a supported V1 rank representation. Unsupported matchups remain visible without a fabricated probability.

Technical detail: display versus exact prediction

Expected margin, median, win probabilities, and intervals come from the exact finite mixture and deterministic mixture quantiles. The chart compresses large mixtures into at most 256 nearby location components for presentation only, then preserves visible-bin and tail mass. Display compression never replaces the exact summary fields.

10

Season simulation hierarchical_latent_state_v1

Status: Shipped in the current team-season pages.

Season Simulation V1 forecasts the remaining regular season from a selected snapshot. It keeps two kinds of uncertainty distinct:

  1. Team-quality uncertainty: sample one rank for every modeled team from its posterior PMF.
  2. Conditional game probabilities: hold those sampled ranks fixed for the entire latent universe and evaluate each future game through Historical Likelihood V1.
  3. Exact conditional win totals: compute each team’s remaining-win distribution with a Poisson-binomial calculation.
  4. Outer average: average those conditional distributions across latent universes.

total season variance = team-quality variance + game-level variance

Production uses hierarchical_latent_state_v1, 2,000 outer draws, zero optional inner rollouts, exact conditional Poisson-binomial win distributions, seed 49,049, and Historical Likelihood V1. Simulated games share one canonical result between the two teams, and simulated outcomes never update the posterior.

The current inference API exposes marginal PMFs, not joint posterior draws. The V1 simulation therefore samples team marginals independently; sampled ranks can repeat and are not forced into a permutation. This is an explicit approximation, not a hidden claim that all teams’ ranks are independent in reality.

Completed regular-season records are fixed at the snapshot. Future and unresolved games enter the forecast scope, while unsupported future games fail closed rather than becoming guessed probabilities.

Technical detail: why rank is sampled once

If team quality were redrawn independently for every future game, the simulation would erase the season-long correlation caused by not knowing how good the team really is. Sampling once per latent universe preserves that shared uncertainty across the team’s remaining schedule. Conditional on the fixed ranks, future game outcomes are treated as independent for the win-total calculation.

Uncertainty is not one thing

The site uses several probability distributions. They answer different questions and should not be substituted for one another.

GippyRank uncertainty layers
DistributionQuestion it answersWhere it appears
Team posteriorHow good might this team be at this snapshot?Rankings and team detail
Game performanceHow strong does this team’s single completed performance look?Team and Schedule completed-game views
Future marginWhat might happen in this scheduled matchup?Team and Schedule future-game views
Season outcomeWhat could this final regular-season win total become?Season outlook on team pages

Central probability intervals describe the middle mass of the named distribution. A wide interval is not a rendering failure; it is the model saying that several outcomes remain plausible. A probability is not certainty, and an expected value is not a guarantee.

12

Historical snapshots and leakage safety

Every published snapshot is intended to mean: what the model knew at that time.

Bundles retain the model and schema identifiers they were generated with. The exporter checks those identifiers against the supported artifact registry and checks internal consistency; it does not require a historical bundle to match today’s current production identifiers.

  • The effective cutoff is the boundary for game evidence. A live request can have a requested cutoff, but the effective cutoff cannot be later than the available source retrieval evidence.
  • Only final, eligible games in the snapshot’s included-game evidence update the posterior.
  • Later games remain future in a historical view. A current schedule score cannot retroactively appear just because its kickoff was before a selected cutoff.
  • Historical future predictions use the posterior available at that historical snapshot.
  • Historical game-performance percentiles use the snapshot-safe reference population, so later performances cannot improve an earlier percentile comparison.
  • The static exporter retains source timestamps, hashes, cutoff values, and included game IDs so the publication can be audited.

The base inference model is season-wide once a cutoff is chosen: the order in which games inside the evidence set are processed does not change the intended model. Chronology matters for deciding which games are known at a snapshot, not as an unannounced time-decay weight.

16

Limitations

These are important boundaries on what the output means.

Model limitations

  • Within a season, the latent quality state is relatively persistent; there is no production time-varying team-state model.
  • The current state is not separated into offense and defense.
  • Schedule quality, team identities, scores, and lower-division support depend on source-data quality.
  • Posterior inference is approximate on cyclic schedules, and only marginal posteriors are exposed.
  • Season simulation uses independent marginal draws as an explicit approximation to the unavailable joint posterior.
  • The likelihood has no explicit injury, roster-availability, weather, or coaching-change adjustment during the season.
  • There is no explicit matchup-style interaction beyond the production rank/site/subdivision likelihood surface.
  • There are no betting-market inputs or poll inputs.

Interpretation limitations

  • Probability is not certainty.
  • Expected rank is not a deterministic ordinal truth or a joint final ordering.
  • A grade is presentation shorthand derived from a percentile.
  • Individual-game performance is not the same thing as team quality.
  • Predictive and Performance rankings answer different questions.
  • A future margin distribution includes game randomness; it is not a promise about a final score.
  • A season-outcome distribution depends on the selected snapshot and its supported schedule scope.
What is not inferred

GippyRank does not infer who deserves a playoff bid, who should be selected, or what a human poll would say. Those are separate judgments.

Reproducibility and source code

The public site is a deterministic static consumer. The publication boundary is:

validated snapshot bundles → static export → compact site data → GitHub Pages

The browser does not run inference, call CFBD, or fetch credentials. The repository keeps raw source data unchanged, transforms it into separate processed artifacts, records source coverage and missingness, and validates publication provenance before export.
